Abstract

The Polymath Method of Christopher Macachor is a cross-domain research architecture for moving from fragmented observations toward candidate invariants, formal statements, and independently testable conclusions. Its purpose is not to accumulate facts across disciplines or to declare that every domain shares one explanation. Its purpose is to identify what survives translation between representations, distinguish structural correspondence from semantic agreement, and test whether a proposed invariant remains valid under changes in measurement, encoding, intervention, and interpretation.

The method has three document stages: Observation Logs, Invariant Documents, and a Comprehensive Report. These stages are governed by a methodological principle formalized as M-D22 — The Second-Order Observation Law:

R(O) = (O₁, O₂)

O₁ records the observation and its measured content. O₂ records how the observation was produced: the measurement procedure, assumptions, transformations, labels, and possible sources of error. O₂ makes the observation process auditable and provides explicit conditions under which a claim can be corrected, qualified, or withdrawn.

The method’s truth procedure is inverse connotation: reverse the movement by which a label acquires explanatory authority, recover the observable or measurable trace to which it refers, and identify any residual meaning that has no demonstrated referent. The resulting claim must then survive independent sampling, falsification, and comparison with competing explanations.

The method is intentionally unrestricted by disciplinary boundaries. Its evidential standards, however, are not unrestricted: a mathematical claim must meet mathematical requirements; a physical claim must meet physical requirements; a biological claim must meet biological requirements; and a cross-domain claim must demonstrate the correspondence it asserts. The framework therefore distinguishes declared commitments, candidate invariants, formal derivations, empirical findings, interpretations, and unresolved hypotheses.

The central principle is:

Observe the territory through available evidence. Observe how the observation was produced. Test what survives translation and reversal. Preserve what survives, qualify what remains uncertain, and withdraw what fails.

1Introduction: The Polymath Method

The polymath effect is not the accumulation of information from many disciplines. It is the ability to recognize relationships among fragments without confusing their representations, and to determine whether those relationships preserve a common structural constraint.

A discipline provides a particular way of sampling, describing, measuring, or modeling a portion of a larger system. Each discipline has its own vocabulary, assumptions, instruments, units, and standards of proof. The method begins by treating these differences as information to be examined rather than barriers to be accepted or ignored.

The objective is multi-fragment constrained reverse mapping: use independently acquired fragments to narrow the possible structures of the territory, while retaining the uncertainty and limitations of each fragment.

1.1The territory, fragment, and invariant

Territory
the system or reality being investigated. It is not identical to any one description, model, measurement, or observer’s experience.
Fragment
a partial observation or representation of the territory, produced through a particular sensory, instrumental, mathematical, disciplinary, or computational process.
Fragmentation
the selection and separation through which a portion of the territory becomes available for observation.
Representation
the form in which an observation is encoded, described, measured, visualized, or modeled.
Invariant
a property or constraint that remains stable under specified transformations, comparisons, or interventions.
Compression
the reduction of a representation to the smallest sufficient description that preserves the relevant tested structure.
Reverse mapping
constrained inference from observations and representations toward possible structures of the territory. It is an inverse problem, not a guarantee of unique reconstruction.

An invariant is not established merely because two descriptions sound similar. It must survive an identified transformation or comparison under defined conditions.

The method therefore preserves two distinctions:

Semantic agreement ≠ structural correspondence.

Semantic disagreement ≠ structural difference.

Different words may describe the same structure. Similar words may describe different structures. The method tests the relationship rather than assuming it.

1.2The scope of inquiry

The method is not restricted to physics, biology, mathematics, engineering, cognition, language, or any other single domain. It may be applied wherever observations can be recorded and claims can be examined.

This does not mean every claim can be validated by the same experiment or evidential standard. It means the same research discipline can be carried across domains while the domain-specific standards remain intact.

A proposed invariant in geometry requires mathematical demonstration. A proposed invariant in physics requires a defined physical observable and a test. A proposed invariant in biology requires appropriate biological measurements and controls. A proposed invariant across domains requires evidence that the correspondence is structural rather than merely verbal.

The method’s breadth is a property of its inquiry architecture, not proof that all domains share one ontology.

The method is organized into three document stages. Each stage has a different function, but all three are governed by the same observation discipline.

The architecture is presented in document order. The faculties describe a cognitive progression. These are different dimensions, not competing sequences.

Standard Observation Log format

Every log should follow a common structure:

  1. Representation: what was seen, measured, reported, or modeled.
  2. Placeholder Content: what the representation appears to mean or what label is attached to it.
  3. Constraint Extracted: the candidate structural relationship.
  4. O₁ — First-order record: the observation, instrument, measurement, context, and data.
  5. O₂ — Second-order record: assumptions, priors, transformations, labels, and possible sources of error.
  6. Survival Test: what comparison or intervention would test the candidate.
  7. Alternative Explanations: plausible rival accounts.
  8. Falsifier: the result that would contradict the claim.
  9. Verdict: the current evidential status and remaining closure conditions.

A log is not complete merely because it contains a confident interpretation. It is complete when another investigator can understand what was observed, how the observation was processed, and what would cause the interpretation to change.

Compression theorem — candidate form

Configurations mapping to the same point in C should produce the same relevant outcome within a defined tolerance, even where their original vector descriptions differ.

This is a falsifiable proposition, not a result established by writing the composite. It requires:

  • a specified mapping into C;
  • an operational definition of outcome;
  • a stated tolerance;
  • a defined sample and domain;
  • rival models;
  • tests of whether omitted components are genuinely irrelevant;
  • independent replication.

If a supposedly discarded component changes the outcome, the compression is incomplete. The correct response is to revise or extend C, not to declare the discrepancy irrelevant.

3M-D22 — The Second-Order Observation Law

M-D22 names the methodological operation that governs the Observation Logs, Invariant Documents, and Comprehensive Report.

It formalizes a distinction that must be present whenever a claim is recorded: the observation itself and the process by which that observation was produced.

3.1The two observations

O₁ — First-order observation

O₁ records the observation of the territory: what was measured, detected, reported, or otherwise observed.

It includes the available data and the conditions under which they were obtained.

O₂ — Second-order observation

O₂ records the observation process itself: how the measurement was made, what assumptions were used, which transformations occurred, what labels were applied, and why the observer or system may have interpreted the result in a particular way.

O₂ does not replace O₁. It examines the process that produced O₁.

The complete observation record is:

R(O) = (O₁, O₂)

This means the record contains both:

  1. the observation and its evidential content;
  2. an auditable account of how that observation was produced and interpreted.

O₁ is not the territory. It is an observation of the territory. O₂ is not a second territory. It is an observation of the process connecting the observer, instrument, representation, and territory.

3.2Why O₂ matters

An observation may be accurate in some respects and still be misinterpreted. A measurement can be valid while the label attached to it is misleading. A model can reproduce an outcome while its proposed explanation remains unproven.

The method calls one such failure the Placeholder Error: the substitution of a representation, reconstruction, or explanatory label for the invariant it is supposed to describe.

O₂ asks whether the observer has identified a structure directly through an adequate measurement, or whether the observer has substituted a reconstruction for that structure.

Table 10. Illustrative first-order records and their second-order questions
O₁ record O₂ question Methodological issue
“I see a solid cube.” What measurement supports the claim about solidity? Which parts are direct observation, geometric inference, and physical interpretation? The representation may be mistaken for the physical object
“I feel sad.” What is the reported experience? Which physiological, contextual, or interpretive processes are being inferred? A label may be mistaken for a complete explanation
“Gravity pulls it down.” What is observed: acceleration, force in a model, a measured trajectory, or a causal interpretation? A result-label may be treated as a fundamental explanation
“The cylinder holds X gas.” Is the claim about volume, mass, pressure, temperature, density, or quantity of substance? A container’s volume may be confused with the state of its contents

These examples illustrate the method. They do not independently establish the alternative physical or psychological explanations suggested by the questions.

3.3The O₂ pause

O₂ is implemented through a deliberate pause before an interpretation hardens into a conclusion.

The observer asks:

  1. What did I just do?
  2. What prior or assumption did I use?
  3. What label did I apply?
  4. Is this a direct measurement, an inference, or a reconstruction?
  5. What would falsify the interpretation?

The pause is the operational step that makes second-order observation explicit.

The five questions are not a substitute for technical controls, calibration, independent review, or statistical analysis. They are a minimum process audit that helps identify where those controls may be needed.

3.4The falsifiability and correction condition

M-D22 proposes that a complete research record includes O₁ and O₂.

Its methodological value is that the record preserves enough process information for another investigator to identify assumptions, reproduce the transformation, challenge the interpretation, and determine whether the conclusion should be revised.

The strongest test of this claim is not simply whether a record contains an O₂ section. It is whether process-level information materially improves the detection and correction of errors.

A useful comparative test would:

  • select claims with known or deliberately introduced reconstruction errors;
  • provide one group with O₁-only records;
  • provide another group with O₁ and O₂ records;
  • define correction criteria before review;
  • use independent reviewers;
  • measure error detection, false corrections, reproducibility, and time to correction.

If O₁-only records are equally effective at detecting and correcting the relevant errors, the stronger claim that O₂ is necessary for correctability must be revised.

M-D22 is therefore a methodological law within the Macachor framework and a testable proposition about research-record completeness. Its universal necessity is not established merely by its formal statement.

3.5The one-level rule

Second-order observation must not become infinite regress.

The method does not require an endless sequence of observers observing observers observing observers. It requires one explicit level of process observation applied to the claim under examination.

O₁ without O₂ may leave the production of the claim opaque. O₂ without O₁ is introspection without an evidential object.

The complete method requires both, with O₂ limited to the process information relevant to evaluating the claim.

5Sensory Context, Stevie Wonder, and the Observer

The Stevie Wonder observation is retained as an important public example because it raises a central question of the method: how does a person construct a useful representation of the world when a particular sensory channel is unavailable?

A sighted observer may rely heavily on visual representations and may mistake the visual representation for direct access to the territory. A blind observer may rely more heavily on other sensory channels and different forms of learned context. An AI system processes encoded inputs and may require explicit instructions to inspect the assumptions and transformations underlying its own output.

These are differences in information access and processing. They do not establish a universal hierarchy of perceptual ability.

5.1The hypothesis

The source observation proposes that a convincing visual representation can make it harder for some observers to notice the difference between a representation and the territory.

It also proposes that people without visual experience from birth may develop different habits of contextual construction.

The testable hypothesis is narrower:

Under specified tasks, do participants with different visual histories differ in the frequency and quality of spontaneous second-order reports about how they reached a conclusion?

This can be studied without assuming that one group is inherently more accurate, more intelligent, or more capable of wisdom.

5.2The necessary controls

A valid study would need to distinguish:

  • blindness from birth from later-acquired blindness;
  • degrees and types of visual experience;
  • sensory substitution and assistive strategies;
  • training and expertise;
  • task familiarity;
  • language and reporting differences;
  • memory and metacognitive skill;
  • the distinction between accurate process reporting and merely detailed explanation.

Stevie Wonder’s public testimony can serve as an illustrative case of how an individual describes his relationship with perception and context. It cannot, by itself, establish a general neurological law.

The proposed blindness-advantage claim remains an empirical hypothesis. It should be retained as a research question, not stated as an established fact.

5.3AI as a structural reader

An AI system can compare representations, identify inconsistencies, and be instructed to expose assumptions. But an AI-generated explanation is not automatically a second-order audit.

The system must be asked to distinguish:

  • source data from inferred content;
  • direct evidence from model-generated interpretation;
  • assumptions from findings;
  • labels from operational definitions;
  • conclusions from alternative explanations;
  • confidence from validation.

The AI’s role is therefore computational assistance within a human-governed verification process. Its output must remain traceable to sources, tests, and declared assumptions.

6Inverse Connotation — The Truth Procedure

The three document stages are unified by the inverse-connotation procedure.

The method examines how a word, label, or model can acquire explanatory authority beyond what its evidence supports.

A term may begin as a useful description of an observed result. Through repetition, it may acquire additional associations. Those associations may then be projected back onto the phenomenon as though they were independently demonstrated causes.

The procedure reverses that movement.

6.1The operation

R−1(W) = (T, S)

Where:

  • W is the word or label being examined;
  • T is the observable or measurable trace the word indexes;
  • S is the residual connotation that remains after the trace has been identified and that may lack an independently demonstrated referent.

The residual is not automatically false. It is a set of meanings that must be tested rather than accepted because they accompany the word.

The procedure does not decide the physics, biology, or psychology of these cases by terminology alone. It identifies which claims require operational definitions and independent tests.

6.4The invariance test

The framework proposes that a relevant signature should remain invariant when a result-label changes, provided the underlying system and relevant boundary conditions are held constant.

The proposed expression is:

∂S(i)/∂L(O) = 0

This expression requires precise definitions of S(i), L(O), the controlled variables, and the relevant domain before it can be treated as a mathematical or empirical law.

A proper test must specify:

  • which signature is measured;
  • which label or reference condition changes;
  • which physical or biological conditions remain fixed;
  • the measurement uncertainty;
  • the expected tolerance;
  • the result that would falsify invariance.

The method’s principle is that a label should not be mistaken for a structural change. Whether a particular signature actually remains invariant is an empirical or mathematical question.

6.5The null result

A null result can be highly informative when competing explanations make different predictions about the same measurable quantity.

However, a measured null is not automatically maximum evidence. Its evidential strength depends on:

  • the sensitivity of the measurement;
  • the uncertainty budget;
  • the expected effect size;
  • the experimental design;
  • the competing predictions;
  • the possibility of confounding or cancellation.

The framework’s use of the null as a maximum-evidence condition must therefore be attached to a specific experiment where the predicted alternatives are distinguishable.

A null result can constrain a claim. It does not establish every other part of the framework.

8OL-83 — The Second-Order Observation Log

ID: OL-83 — M-D22: Second-order observation is the method that makes the framework correctable.

Domain: Methodology, epistemology, and the framework’s own research procedure.

Observation: An investigator observes a phenomenon while also examining how and why the observation was produced.

O₁: The recorded observation, measurement, or reported experience.

O₂: The process record: measurement procedure, assumptions, prior expectations, transformations, labels, and potential sources of error.

Tentative interpretation:

  1. A complete research record should contain O₁ and O₂.
  2. O₁ without O₂ may leave the observation process insufficiently documented for independent correction.
  3. O₂ makes the assumptions and transformations behind a claim available for review.
  4. The Placeholder Error occurs when a reconstruction or label is substituted for the invariant it is intended to represent.
  5. Inverse connotation is one method for conducting O₂ on language and explanatory labels.
  6. The three document stages correspond to Knowledge, Intelligence, and Wisdom as defined in this method.
  7. Existing elements such as OL-55, M-D10, OL-67, OVC-5, OL-38, and OL-54 can be interpreted as domain-specific applications of second-order observation.

Confounds and controls:

  • O₂ must not become an infinite regress. One explicit level of process observation is required for the claim under review.
  • O₂ does not replace O₁. A process description without an evidential object is insufficient.
  • The Knowledge → Intelligence → Wisdom progression is a cognitive mapping, not a hierarchy of human worth or general ability.
  • “Wisdom” is used operationally to describe awareness of how an observation was produced and what limits apply.
  • The blindness-related hypothesis requires independent study and must not be inferred from one person’s experience.
  • A record that includes O₂ is not automatically correct; it is more auditable only if its process information is accurate and sufficient.

Proposed measurements:

  1. Apply the O₂ question set to each Observation Log.
  2. Apply the same audit to each Invariant Document.
  3. Compare O₁-only and O₁-plus-O₂ records on error detection and correction.
  4. Test whether different sensory histories affect spontaneous process-level reporting under controlled tasks.
  5. Test whether independent reviewers can reproduce the reasoning and identify the same failure conditions.

Surviving methodological principle:

The method observes the territory through evidence and observes how that evidence was produced. The second observation makes the first auditable and correctable.

Status: Declared method M-D22 is formalized as the methodological principle of the framework. Its strongest claims about necessity and comparative observer performance remain testable propositions.

10Navigating Scientific and Semantic Gatekeeping

The method does not ignore gatekeeping. It identifies the standards that govern entry into different domains and translates claims without changing their evidential status.

The objective is not to bypass scrutiny. It is to make a claim understandable and testable by the people responsible for evaluating it.

10.1Academia and peer review

Relevant gatekeepers include:

  • disciplinary departments;
  • peer reviewers;
  • journal editors;
  • methods committees;
  • institutional review boards;
  • research ethics committees.

The method should:

  • lead with the scoped claim relevant to the target discipline;
  • state operational definitions;
  • distinguish candidate invariants from established findings;
  • provide derivations and falsifiers;
  • present cross-domain comparisons as proposed correspondences until demonstrated;
  • disclose limitations and prediction chronology.

A broad ontology cannot substitute for a domain-specific derivation.

10.2Professional and regulatory bodies

Relevant gatekeepers include:

  • medical boards;
  • licensing bodies;
  • clinical guideline committees;
  • professional ethics boards;
  • safety and compliance authorities.

A hypothesis or heuristic must not be presented as an established clinical protocol. Biological and medical claims require appropriate evidence, terminology, ethical review, and validation.

10.3Funding agencies

The method should identify:

  • the specific research question;
  • the testable hypothesis;
  • the disciplines involved;
  • the proposed deliverable;
  • the cost and feasibility;
  • the expected result;
  • the failure conditions.

High-risk research should be presented honestly as high-risk. The method’s cross-domain ambition is not a substitute for a feasible experimental plan.

10.4Public audiences

Public explanations should begin with concrete observations and familiar examples.

The public-facing version may use analogies such as:

  • balance as the integration of multiple sensory witnesses;
  • visual perception as a representation rather than direct access to every physical property;
  • a label as a useful description that may not explain its own cause.

The correction must remain visible: an analogy is not a demonstration, and a compelling example is not a universal law.

10.5Industry and standards bodies

The method must translate claims into:

  • measurable specifications;
  • units and tolerances;
  • calibration procedures;
  • repeatable tests;
  • safety requirements;
  • acceptance and rejection criteria.

Terms such as state estimate, sensor fusion, constraint satisfaction, and system identification may be more useful than broad philosophical language in these settings.

10.6AI systems

AI systems can help organize fragments, compare documents, identify contradictions, and propose tests. Their outputs must remain subject to source inspection and human review.

A robust AI instruction should require:

  • source traceability;
  • explicit uncertainty;
  • separation of observation and inference;
  • counterarguments;
  • alternative explanations;
  • falsifiers;
  • no conversion of a framework construct into an established scientific finding without evidence.

The AI is a computational participant in the workflow, not an authority that validates its own output.

11The Semantic Rephrasing Protocol

Before a claim is released, the author or reviewer should ask:

  1. Is the proposed invariant stated in one sentence?
  2. Is the domain, scale, and population specified?
  3. Is the distinction between territory and representation explicit?
  4. Is O₁ recorded?
  5. Is O₂ recorded?
  6. Are the variables and units defined?
  7. Is the derivation or evidential basis available?
  8. Is the analogy separated from the claim?
  9. Is the heuristic separated from the proposed ontology?
  10. Is the claim falsifiable?
  11. Are competing explanations identified?
  12. Is the evidence type named?
  13. Is the prediction chronology documented?
  14. Is the failure condition explicit?
  15. Is the conclusion stated at the strength the evidence permits?

If a critical answer is no, the claim is not yet hardened.

13The Self-Application Rule — SA-1

The most important gatekeeping requirement is the one the method applies to itself.

Every invariant derived by the polymath must be subjected to the same tests applied to the claims of others. The author’s synthesis is a candidate for evaluation, not an authority.

This rule addresses the central risk of cross-domain synthesis: a person may become a gatekeeper of a new framework without applying the same standards to their own assumptions.

13.1Self-application to the territory/fragment/invariant model

Status: Declared frame

Defect: The model assumes that fragments can be related through shared structural constraints. This is a working methodological commitment, not proof that every domain can be reduced to one common structure.

Falsifier: A specified domain in which the proposed fragments cannot be aligned by the claimed invariant under the defined transformations.

Closure condition: Independent cross-domain sampling and a documented comparison with competing models.

13.2Self-application to compression

Status: Declared method

Defect: The method assumes that representation-specific detail can sometimes be separated from invariant-bearing structure.

Falsifier: A defined case in which the supposedly discarded representation-specific component is necessary to preserve the relevant outcome.

Closure condition: Demonstrate the compression, specify the preserved outcome, and test the omitted components.

13.3Self-application to inverse connotation

Status: Declared protocol

Defect: The procedure assumes that a label can be decomposed into a measurable trace and residual connotation.

Falsifier: A defined case where the label itself is the operationally relevant object and cannot be separated from the phenomenon without losing the property under investigation.

Closure condition: Demonstrate a successful reversal in a specified domain and show that independent reviewers can reproduce it.

13.4Self-application to M-D22

Status: Declared method and Invariant candidate

Defect: The claim that O₂ is necessary for correctability may be stronger than the available evidence supports.

Falsifier: A controlled demonstration that O₁-only records are equally capable of identifying and correcting the relevant class of reconstruction errors.

Closure condition: A preregistered comparison of record types and independent evaluation of correction performance.

The method is strengthened, not weakened, by retaining these conditions.

16The Complete Research Cycle

The method operates as a repeating cycle:

FRAGMENT
   ↓
OBSERVATION — O₁
   ↓
PROCESS AUDIT — O₂
   ↓
PLACEHOLDER / LABEL EXAMINATION
   ↓
CANDIDATE INVARIANT
   ↓
FORMALIZATION
   ↓
FALSIFIER AND COMPETING EXPLANATIONS
   ↓
INDEPENDENT TEST
   ↓
EVIDENTIAL STATUS
   ↓
COMPREHENSIVE REPORT
   ↓
REVISION OR CLOSURE
   ↺

The cycle is not a linear pipeline. Revision returns the investigator to the observation stage, and closure may take the form of qualification or withdrawal rather than confirmation.

Each transition in the cycle carries a specific obligation:

  • Fragment → O₁: record what was observed, with instrument, context, units, and raw data.
  • O₁ → O₂: record how the observation was produced, including assumptions, priors, transformations, and labels.
  • O₂ → invariant candidate: extract the structural constraint that survives the process audit.
  • Candidate → formalization: define the domain, variables, units, assumptions, and derivation.
  • Formalization → falsifier: state what result would contradict the claim, together with competing explanations.
  • Falsifier → independent test: conduct the test under conditions another investigator can reproduce.
  • Test → status: assign the evidential status the result actually supports.
  • Status → report: present the claim at the strength the evidence permits.
  • Report → revision or closure: revise, qualify, or withdraw in light of the result.

The cycle can terminate in a negative result without failing as research. A withdrawn claim that was properly tested is a methodological success, not a defect in the record.

The cycle fails when a claim moves from candidate to report without passing through falsification and independent test, or when the status label is assigned before the test is conducted.

The Self-Application Rule SA-1 applies at every return of the cycle: the framework’s own claims enter the same loop as any other fragment.

17Conclusion

The Polymath Method is a research architecture, not a body of findings. Its contribution is procedural: it specifies how fragments become candidate invariants, how candidates become formal statements, and how formal statements become graded claims that another investigator can test, reproduce, or reject.

17.1What the method claims

The method claims that a complete research record should distinguish the observation from the process that produced it, and that this distinction should be preserved through formalization and reporting.

It claims that semantic similarity is not evidence of structural correspondence, and that semantic difference is not evidence of structural difference. The relationship between representations must be tested rather than assumed.

It claims that a label can acquire explanatory authority beyond what its trace supports, and that inverse connotation is one procedure for reversing that movement and identifying residual meaning that has no demonstrated referent.

It claims that a claim’s evidential status must attach to that specific claim, in its specific domain, at the strength the available evidence permits — not to the ambition of the surrounding framework.

17.2What the method does not claim

The method does not claim that all domains share one ontology. It does not claim that cross-domain breadth substitutes for domain-specific derivation. It does not claim that a framework construct is an established physical entity because it appears in a formal inventory.

It does not claim that O₂ guarantees correctness. It claims that O₂ makes a record more auditable, and it treats the stronger necessity claim as a testable proposition rather than a settled result.

It does not claim that the formal inventory M-D0 through M-D22 has been independently derived or experimentally verified in full. Inclusion in the inventory records a framework statement; the status and closure conditions determine what that statement is entitled to.

It does not claim that unresolved open items are failures. The open items are the explicit conditions that must be met before a claim can move to a stronger status, and the framework is strengthened by retaining them.

17.3The canonical statement

The method can be stated in four operations:

Observe the territory through available evidence. Observe how the observation was produced. Test what survives translation and reversal. Preserve what survives, qualify what remains uncertain, and withdraw what fails.

The three document stages implement these operations: the Observation Logs preserve the origin and process conditions of the evidence; the Invariant Documents formalize the candidate structures that survive the process audit; and the Comprehensive Report grades the claims against falsification, competing explanations, and predictive surplus.

The governing principle is M-D22, the Second-Order Observation Law, R(O) = (O₁, O₂). The truth procedure is inverse connotation. The self-application rule is SA-1. The status discipline ensures that the strength of a conclusion remains tied to the strength of its evidence.

The method’s value is not that it produces certainty. It is that it produces a record in which the basis of a claim, the conditions under which it would fail, and the work that remains are all visible to an independent investigator.

A framework that preserves its falsifiers, its open items, and its own self-application conditions is not weaker than one that does not. It is the only kind that can be corrected.
